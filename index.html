Five steps. Ten minutes. It argues back.

You already know you should narrow. This is the part nobody helps with.

01

The dump

Every audience you've ever told yourself you serve, including the ones you'd be embarrassed to say out loud. You can't subtract from a short list.

02

The reality check

Have you talked to five of them in 90 days? Can you name one from memory, right now? Would they pay today? Scored on evidence, not on which you like most.

03

The cut

Keep one. For every other, type what you're giving up. You cannot continue until each cost is written down — that's what makes the decision survive a hard week.

04

The gauntlet

Rewrite until the page stops arguing. Vague nouns, hedge words, and two-audiences-in-one-sentence each get named and quoted back to you.

05

The consequences

Who this is deliberately not for. What they believe that others don't. The person before, and the person after. Out comes a card you can put on a wall.

Built around the step everyone skips

Seth Godin describes marketing in five steps. This tool serves two of them, and says so.

  1. Invent a thing worth making, with a story worth telling.
  2. Build it so that a few people particularly benefit and care.
  3. Tell a story that matches the built-in narrative and dreams of that tiny group — the smallest viable market.
  4. Spread the word — the step everybody gets excited about.
  5. Show up regularly, consistently and generously, for years.

Paraphrased from This Is Marketing by Seth Godin. Steps two and three both depend on an act most people never perform: deciding who they are not for. A tool claiming all five would serve none of them.
