Nobody wants to read your words. They want to read theirs.

Most landing pages fail in a boring way: they're written in the founder's vocabulary, for an audience that uses a completely different one. You wrote streamline your workflow. They said I lose a whole day to invoicing. Same problem. Only one of those sentences sells.

Scout helps creative professionals streamline their workflow and unlock their full potential with AI-powered insights.

FLAG  “Streamline their workflow” — zero people in this audience have ever used that phrase. Fourteen said a version of “I lose a whole day to invoicing.”

ASK  Who is this for, specifically? “Creative professionals” covers a fourteen-year-old with an iPad and a studio of forty.

ASK  What change are you trying to make? Name the person before and the person after.

Red Pen stops here. It has no rewrite button, no “stronger version,” no autocomplete. The words have to be yours or they're not worth anything.

How it works

Set it up in a few minutes. After that, involvement is one email a week.

01

Name the group

One sentence. “Freelance illustrators who just went full-time.” Scout proposes the ten or twenty places those people actually gather — forums, threads, Q&A sites, comment sections. You confirm and edit.

02

Scout listens

It reads them on a schedule and keeps one page current: their words, their tensions, their dreams, what they're against, and the things they've asked for that nobody built.

03

Red Pen judges you

Paste your homepage, your launch email, your pitch. It marks every phrase your audience has never said, asks the questions you skipped, and then leaves the writing to you.

A quiet week says “nothing shifted” in one line and stops. Scout is not allowed to invent an insight to justify the email.

Built around the step everyone skips

Seth Godin describes marketing in five steps. Four of them have a hundred tools. One doesn't.

  1. Invent a thing worth making, with a story worth telling.
  2. Build it so that a few people particularly benefit and care.
  3. Tell a story that matches the built-in narrative and dreams of that tiny group — the smallest viable market. This is what Scout is for.
  4. Spread the word — the step everybody gets excited about.
  5. Show up regularly, consistently and generously, for years.

Paraphrased from This Is Marketing by Seth Godin. Step three is the one you can't do from a chair — it needs the actual words of actual people. That's the gap.
