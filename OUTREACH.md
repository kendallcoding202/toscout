# Stage 1 — the first five

Everything needed to get Pick One in front of the people who'd buy the paid version. No ads,
no launch, no list. Five conversations.

**The only number that matters right now:** how many of the first five *finish* the tool.
Under three, stop marketing and fix the mechanic — nothing downstream works if people quit at
the cut.

---

## Who exactly

You are looking for one specific person: **someone who runs positioning or messaging sessions
for clients, and is doing it on a Miro board.** Not marketers in general. Not founders.

The tell is that they post publicly about *client* work — a workshop they ran, a messaging
framework they built, a repositioning they led. That means they have deadlines, deliverables,
and a reason to try a tool this week rather than someday.

| Where | Exactly what to search | Why them |
|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | `positioning consultant` · `messaging strategist` · `fractional product marketing` · `positioning workshop` | Highest concentration anywhere. They post client work publicly, which is exactly the signal you need |
| **Product Marketing Alliance** | Their Slack (30k+ members), positioning and messaging channels | The single densest gathering of people who do this for a living |
| **Sharebird** | The Product Marketing hub; look at who answers positioning questions | Free, and the people writing detailed answers are practitioners, not lurkers |
| **RevGenius** | Free Slack, marketing channels | Cross-functional GTM crowd; consultants hang out here |
| **Fractional CMO groups** | The Facebook group of that name; also `fractional CMO` on LinkedIn | Fractionals run positioning sessions constantly and buy their own tools |
| **Maven** | Course listings for positioning / messaging / PMM | Instructors are this buyer *and* they're publicly listed with contact details |
| **Contra / Upwork** | Filter freelancers by `positioning`, `messaging`, `brand strategy` | Public profiles, filterable, and they state their specialty |
| **April Dunford's orbit** | People whose bio mentions `Obviously Awesome` or that they're trained in her method | They self-identify as doing exactly this work |

**How to pick the five.** Prefer anyone who posted about a live client project in the last 30
days. A deadline is the difference between "interesting, saved it" and someone actually
opening the tool.

**Do not start with:** founders, indie hackers, Product Hunt, or your friends. Founders are the
free tier — they'll use it and never pay. Friends will be nice to you, which is worse than
useless.

## Two messages, depending on who they are

**The rule: never critique a professional's copy.** A conversion copywriter with ten years'
experience does not need a script to tell them their homepage is vague, and sending one reads
as arrogant from a stranger. Split the list:

| They are | The move |
|---|---|
| A founder, an in-house PMM, an agency generalist | Run the tool on their homepage line, send the rejection |
| **A positioning or messaging practitioner** | Don't critique them. Show them the tool and ask them to judge it |

The second group is the one that buys. Treat them as the expert they are — the ask is their
professional opinion, which is flattering, cheap to give, and gets replies.

---

### Message A — for practitioners (the buyers)

Do not ask for their time. Do not describe the product. Reference the specific thing they
sell, show the tool doing its one trick, and ask one blunt question.

> **Subject: your homepage says "ambitious teams"**
>
> Hi [name] — saw your post about the [client type] repositioning.
>
> I built a small thing that argues with audience sentences. I fed it the line from your
> homepage — *"we help ambitious teams scale faster"* — and it refused it:
>
> > "teams" on its own describes millions of people. What kind, at what moment?
> > Nothing here says what makes them different from everyone else with that job title.
>
> It's free, no signup, runs entirely in the browser, takes about ten minutes: [link]
>
> One question if you try it: did you finish it, or did you bail? Either answer is useful —
> I'd rather know it's annoying than not know.
>
> [you]

**Why this works:** you're doing them a favour before asking for anything, the observation is
about *them*, and the ask is one question they can answer in four words.

## What to watch, and what it means

Ask nothing except whether they finished. Then watch the pattern.

| What happens | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Finishes, then asks if they can use it with a client | The strongest signal available | Build the paid tier now |
| Finishes, says "it's annoying but it's right" | Working exactly as designed | Keep going, ask what they'd pay |
| Quits at step 3 (the cut) | The cost-writing gate is too heavy, or the stakes aren't clear | Fix the mechanic before anything else |
| Quits at step 4 (the gauntlet) | It's rejecting sentences that are actually fine | Get the exact sentence. That's a bug, not a user error |
| "Nice, thanks" and nothing else | Polite disinterest. The commonest outcome | Two more of these and reconsider the buyer |
| Sends it to someone else unprompted | It's shareable, which is the whole distribution plan | Ask who they sent it to and why |

Keep a plain list: name, where you found them, finished yes/no, where they stopped, exact
words. Five rows. That list is worth more than anything else in this repo.

## After the five

- **Three or more finished** → publish the stage-2 artifact: run 50 public homepages through it
  and write up how many describe an audience that excludes nobody. Anonymize them — publicly
  dunking on named companies' copy loses you the exact people you want as customers. Post it
  where you found the five.
- **Fewer than three finished** → do not market. Fix the step they quit at, find five more
  people, repeat. Marketing a tool people don't finish just buys a bigger sample of the same
  answer.

## The share button

Every rejection and every pass has a **Copy this** button that puts the exchange on the
clipboard, ready to paste. Nothing is sent anywhere — it's the user's own clipboard.

That's the loop: the tool argues with someone, they paste the argument somewhere public,
and the argument is more interesting than any ad you could write about it.
